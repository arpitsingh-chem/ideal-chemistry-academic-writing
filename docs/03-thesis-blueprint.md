# 03 · The Ideal Chemistry PhD Thesis — Blueprint (Indian University Standard)

Based on UGC norms, IISER thesis practice, and the publishing standards of the chemistry portfolio (journals' characterisation and citation standards carry directly into thesis chapters).

> ⚠️ **Always confirm with your institute's Academic Office / thesis cell**: preliminary-page wording, similarity threshold (typically **≤10%** core area at IISERs, UGC policy), and submission workflow. This blueprint matches the common standard.

---

## 1. Preliminary pages (in this exact order)

1. **Title page** — thesis title, candidate name, degree, department, institute, year; institute logo; no page number (counted but not shown).
2. **Certificate** — supervisor's certification (institute format).
3. **Declaration** — candidate's declaration of originality, with plagiarism-check statement.
4. **Acknowledgements** — personal + funding; keep professional; no gossip.
5. **Abstract** — 250–350 words; standalone; mirrors a journal abstract's four moves.
6. **Table of contents** — with page numbers; chapters + sections.
7. **List of figures / tables / schemes** — numbered, titled, page-referenced.
8. **Abbreviations & symbols** — chemistry-heavy theses need a comprehensive list (include NMR solvent conventions, IR units, etc.).

## 2. Core chapters

### Chapter 1 — Introduction & Literature Review
- Field context → knowledge gap → thesis objectives → thesis organisation.
- Review organised **thematically** (methods → classes of compounds → properties), not chronologically.
- Every claim cited; include the last 3–5 years of literature.
- End with explicit **objectives** and the **approach** used.

### Chapter 2 — Materials and Methods (or per-chapter experimental)
- General: reagents, solvents, instruments, standard procedures.
- If per-chapter: keep a **shared general methods** section here; repeat only deviations.
- Include full characterisation data standards from `docs/02` §8 — thesis examiners are journal reviewers.

### Chapters 3–5 — Results (one story per chapter)
Each results chapter is a **paper-shaped unit**:
- Brief chapter-specific introduction (1–2 pages, re-citing the essentials — a thesis is allowed to repeat background that journals would not).
- Results with figures/tables/schemes; numbered continuously through the thesis (Figure 3.1, Scheme 4.2…).
- Chapter-end summary + outlook.

### Final chapter — Conclusions and Future Directions
- Consolidated conclusions (numbered, data-backed).
- Honest limitations.
- Future directions (3–6 concrete ideas).

## 3. Back matter
- **Bibliography** — one consistent style (thesis manuals often specify ACS or numbered style; be consistent chapter-to-chapter).
- **Appendices** — spectra compilations, crystallographic tables, publication list ("List of Publications" — *from thesis work*, often required), conference papers, reprints permission notes.

---

## 4. Formatting standard (typical Indian university)

| Item | Standard |
|------|----------|
| Paper | A4 |
| Margins | Left 1.5 in (binding), others 1 in |
| Font | Times New Roman 12 pt (or 11 pt) body; 1.5 line spacing; single for long quotes/footnotes |
| Headings | Consistent hierarchy (numbered), bold |
| Figures | ≥300 dpi; numbered + captioned; referenced in text |
| Equations | Numbered, right-aligned; ChemDraw ≥600 dpi for structures |
| Page numbers | Bottom centre or top right; preliminary pages in roman numerals, main text in Arabic |
| Chapters | Start on new page |

## 5. Originality & similarity (the part students get wrong)

- UGC/Indian-institution norm: **similarity index ≤10%** (references/bibliography/acknowledgements excluded by the check).
- **Why theses get flagged:** copying your *own published papers* verbatim (self-plagiarism), copying group-mates' experimental descriptions, and pasting review paragraphs.
- **The fix (from `docs/09`):**
  - Write every chapter in your own words, including your own papers' methods (rephrase the experimental text even for your own work; cite the paper).
  - Keep **drafts with version history** (Overleaf/Git/Google Docs) as authorship evidence.
  - Run the institute's similarity tool on a draft *early*; fix flagged passages; re-check before final submission.
  - Quoted text: short, quoted, cited — the tool excludes attributed quotes in most policies.

## 6. Thesis × publications overlap (legitimate, done right)

Publishing chapters as papers *before* submission is normal and encouraged; a thesis need not be textually identical:
- Reuse *figures and data* (with institute permission; cite the paper in the thesis).
- **Rewrite the prose** — thesis language (first person, expanded context) differs from journal language anyway.
- List the publications in the back matter; mention them in the introduction ("parts of this work have been published: …").
